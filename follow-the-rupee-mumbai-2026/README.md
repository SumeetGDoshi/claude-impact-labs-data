# Follow the Rupee: Mumbai Monsoon Roads dataset

Dataset for the **Claude Impact Lab, Mumbai (21 Nov 2026)**. Theme: **accountability and budgeting**.

> ⚠️ **Everything here is synthetic.** It was generated by a script to look and behave like real Mumbai civic data. It is **not BMC data**, has no real citizens in it, and must not be used to judge any real person, contractor or department. **All contractors are fictional.** Ward codes, locality names, arterial road names, well-known flooding spots and major public hospitals are real names, used only for realism. Every coordinate, boundary, amount, date and record is made up.

## The problem

Every monsoon, Mumbai spends crores fixing the same roads. Citizens complain, repairs get logged, and the potholes come back. Nobody can easily answer three questions: **where did the money go, did the fix last, and who is responsible?**

**Build a Claude-powered tool that shows where each ward's road repair money went, whether the work held up, and where the next rupee should go.**

A good solution will:

1. **Connect** complaints to repair work. Messy, duplicated, multilingual complaints become clean issues, linked to the work orders that fixed them (or didn't).
2. **Audit** the repairs. Missed SLAs, cost per sqm against the rate card, fixes that re-opened within 30 days, suspicious bills.
3. **Hold to account.** A fair, plain-language scorecard for each contractor and each ward that a citizen or a corporator can read.
4. **Budget smarter.** Recommend where each ward should spend next, based on risk: rain, flooding, tides, traffic, accidents, schools and hospitals nearby.

Complaint clean-up (de-duplication, multilingual parsing, geocoding) is a supporting step, not the goal.

## What's inside

```
data/
  wards.csv / wards.geojson   24 BMC wards (A to T), approximate boundaries, monsoon repair budget
  work_orders.csv             2,499 repair jobs: contractor, method, area, cost, SLA, re-opened
  rate_card.csv               Standard rate per repair method (per sqm or per job)
  contractors.csv             8 fictional contractors
  complaints.csv              6,501 MyBMC app, 1916 helpline, portal and walk-in complaints (Jun to Sep 2025)
  complaints_sample_200.csv   200 complaints from a heavy-rain spell (7 to 8 Jul) for quick prompting
  social_posts.csv            650 X / WhatsApp / Instagram posts, mixed languages, with noise
  roads.csv / roads.geojson   291 road segments, incl. last resurfaced date and DLP status
  localities.csv              118 localities with lat/lon and aliases (incl. Devanagari)
  flood_hotspots.csv          28 chronic waterlogging spots
  rainfall_daily.csv          Daily IMD-style rainfall, Santacruz and Colaba
  tides.csv                   Daily high tides, big-tide flag (>= 4.5 m)
  traffic.csv                 Peak hour volume, speed, congestion per road segment
  accidents.csv               420 road accidents (2024 to 2025) with probable cause
  facilities.csv              Hospitals and municipal schools with nearest road
DATA_DICTIONARY.md            Every column explained
STARTER_IDEAS.md              Build ideas, prompts and a judging rubric
```

## How the money trail connects

```
complaints ──(linked_complaint_ids)──> work_orders ──(road_id)──> roads (DLP, resurfaced)
                                           │
                     contractor_id ────────┼──── repair_method ──> rate_card
                                           │
                                       ward_code ──> wards (budget)
```

## Quick start

```python
import pandas as pd
wo = pd.read_csv("data/work_orders.csv")

print(wo.groupby("contractor_name")[["cost_inr"]].sum())
```

Then hand Claude one contractor's work orders and the rate card, and ask it to write a fair one-paragraph scorecard. That's your first prototype.

## Scale note

Amounts are realistic per job (a pothole patch costs thousands of rupees, a cave-in repair a couple of lakh), but total spend across the city is about ₹13 crore for the season. That is smaller than real BMC road spending, because only complaint-driven monsoon repairs are included. Ward budgets in `wards.csv` are sized against this spend, so comparisons between budget and spend work.
